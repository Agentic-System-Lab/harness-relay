# Meeting notes

> **Status: not started; a survey, and the design it recommends.** See [Proposals](README.md).

What it would take for Harness Relay to do what [Grok Bot's meetings](https://docs.x.ai/grok-bot/meetings) do: a team that takes notes of the user's Google Meet, Zoom and Microsoft Teams calls, answers questions about a call while it goes on, and gives a summary and action items once it ends, which it can then send on. And which routes into a meeting there are for a program on the user's own computer with no public address, and what each would ask of Harness Relay's defences.

## How this was found

A survey on 2026-11-23 of Harness Relay's code, of xAI's Grok Bot documentation, and of the documentation, changelogs and developer forums of Microsoft, Google, Zoom, Apple, Electron, whisper.cpp and the meeting bot services, checked again by a reviewer who had not done it (the code's claims against the code, the load-bearing sources fetched again, every link opened). Nothing was installed, signed in to or run, but for reading the installed desktop app's Info.plist. Each finding about another product has a label:

- **docs**: in its maker's documentation, changelog, announcements or published code;
- **reported**: only in the press, a forum (the maker's own included) or a third party's write-up;
- **unsure**: not confirmed; to be checked before anything is built on it.

What is said about Harness Relay itself cites its file, and has no label.

## What Grok Bot does

From [its meetings page](https://docs.x.ai/grok-bot/meetings) and the pages next to it (**docs**; several of those pages still carry another product's names and addresses, so these are the docs' claims, not behaviour seen):

- It joins a Google Meet, Zoom or Teams call as a participant named "*name* (*your first name*'s Bot) - transcribing", whom the host admits. "It listens, takes notes, answers questions in the meeting chat, and sends you a summary and action items when the call ends." Nothing says it speaks.
- It is sent three ways: a meeting link pasted into its chat (and a card approved), its following the user's calendar, or a routine (whose bot takes notes but does not answer in the chat).
- Each meeting has Disable Interactivity (notes only; before it joins), Cancel and Quit. A past meeting's summary and transcript can be asked for later; a long report goes on a page.
- When someone in the meeting asks for something it held back, it tells its owner who asked and offers to send it; it never shares the owner's credentials in a meeting.
- An admin turns meetings off for a whole organization. No API, recording vendor, consent notice (beyond the name), limits or languages are documented.

## What exists today

- **Voice input** (`src/server/voice.ts`): whisper.cpp's `whisper-server`, installed from Settings (from Homebrew on macOS; elsewhere a pinned release, checked by SHA-256, as each model is), started on 127.0.0.1 on a random port under a random path prefix with `--no-timestamps`, and stopped when unused. It takes one recording of up to 120 seconds at a time (`VOICE_MAX_SECONDS` in `src/shared/types.ts`), which the page sends as a base64 WAV in JSON (`POST /api/voice/transcribe`, within `VOICE_MAX_BASE64` in `src/server/app.ts`); for Chinese, with a prompt that has it written in Traditional characters (`whisperLanguage()`). The page records the microphone alone (`src/web/recorder.ts`).
- **The desktop app's media** (`permissionAllowed()` in `desktop/src/lifecycle.ts`): the server's own pages may have the microphone, and no other media. Its resource-access entitlements are the microphone's and Apple Events' (`desktop/entitlements.mac.plist`); Harness Relay sets the microphone's and Apple Events' usage descriptions (`extendInfo` in `desktop/electron-builder.yml`, translated in `desktop/macos/*.lproj/InfoPlist.strings`), and Electron's own Info.plist adds generic ones, `NSAudioCaptureUsageDescription` ("This app needs access to audio capture") among them, as the installed app shows. It runs Electron 45 and is signed ad hoc.
- **Calendars** (`CalendarEvent` in `src/server/connectors/caldav.ts`, `graphEvent()` in `graph.ts`): an event's title, times, location, description and attendees, but not its online meeting. Graph's `onlineMeeting`, `isOnlineMeeting` and `onlineMeetingProvider` are not asked for, and its description is only the start of the body (`bodyPreview`); iCalendar's `CONFERENCE` is not read. A meeting link is there only when it is in the location or that description.
- **An automation that starts before an event** (`checkCalendar()` in `src/server/watch.ts`): the events about to begin, each once, as an `<event>` block of data. Nothing starts when an event ends.
- **A Microsoft account that signs in with OAuth** (`microsoftProvider()` in `src/server/oauth/providers.ts`): Graph's calendar (`Calendars.ReadWrite`) and Teams reads (`Team.ReadBasic.All`, `Channel.ReadBasic.All`, `ChannelMessage.Read.All`). No meeting, transcript or recording scope.
- **Teams posts** (`src/server/connectors/teams.ts`, `messaging.ts`): through Microsoft Graph PowerShell's posting session (`ChatMessage.Send`, `ChannelMessage.Send`), `teams_send_message` proposes a message for a chat or channel named by a copied message link. It is a `send` tool, so it waits in the outbox for the user's confirmation in every permission. A Teams meeting's chat is a chat, but `destinationFromLink()` refuses a meeting's join link (`/l/meetup-join/…`), although a classic one holds the chat's thread (**unsure**: posting not tried with a meeting's chat).
- **Google Drive** (`src/server/connectors/apps.ts`): `drive_search` and `drive_read_document` (`drive.readonly`) could already read the transcripts and Gemini notes Meet saves as Docs, where the account may open them (**unsure**: not tried with those Docs).
- **Text from elsewhere**: a tool's result from outside is given to agents in a block that says it is data, whose tags a text inside cannot close (`dataBody()` in `src/server/gateway.ts`); RLM mode (`src/server/runCode.ts`, Settings → Labs) reads a long text in parts.
- **The browser tools** (`src/server/browser/`): a headless Chromium per conversation with a profile of its own, every connection through Harness Relay's proxy, WebRTC kept to it with no UDP of its own (`--force-webrtc-ip-handling-policy=disable_non_proxied_udp`), no microphone, its sound muted (`--mute-audio`).

## What is missing

- Capturing what the others in a call say (the meeting app's sound), on any system.
- Transcribing a long recording as it goes, and keeping the transcript: who said what, and when.
- A meeting as something a conversation has: its card and controls, its transcript given to agents, its summary.
- A calendar event's meeting link and meeting (Teams' online meeting, Meet's conference), and anything that happens once a meeting ends.

## The routes into a meeting

| Route | Platforms | Live | What it needs | What stands in the way |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A. **On this computer**: the user's microphone and the meeting's sound, transcribed by whisper.cpp | every app, and meetings in person | yes | the desktop app's system audio permission, or a tab shared in Chrome or Edge | the user's own computer, and the user there; telling the others is the user's to do |
| B. **The platform's transcript**, read after the meeting | Teams, Meet, Zoom | no | work or school accounts with transcription on; the scopes below | admin consent (Teams); the organizer or host only (Meet's list, Zoom); minutes to hours late |
| C. **A bot service's participant** (Recall.ai, Attendee, Vexa, Meeting BaaS, Skribby) | all three | only with a public endpoint | an account and a key; $0.30–0.50 an hour, transcription extra | everyone's audio to a third party; 2026's rules for bots (below) |
| D. **A bot of Harness Relay's own**: the browser tools, or one like joinly | Meet, Teams | yes | a browser with media, signed in | as C, and the browser tools' defences |
| E. **The platforms' live APIs** | — | yes | a public URL (Zoom RTMS's webhook), Windows Server media bots in Azure (Teams), the developer preview's enrollment (Meet Media API) | none reaches a server on 127.0.0.1; Meet's Media API ends on 2027-01-15 |

- Ad hoc signing: macOS ties a privacy grant to the app's signature, so an ad hoc signed app loses its grants at each update (**reported**, Apple's developer support on its forums, about Screen Recording); whether the system audio grant goes the same way is **unsure**.
- Me and them: the microphone and the meeting's sound captured apart tell the user from the others, as Granola and OpenWhispr do (**reported**); whisper.cpp's `--diarize` labels a stereo recording's two channels as two speakers (**docs**). Without headphones the microphone hears the others too, and Chromium's echo cancellation removes only what Chromium itself plays (**unsure**).
- Transcribing as it goes: `whisper-server` has no streaming endpoint, only `/inference` for a file, and takes `--vad` (Silero, with a model file of its own) (**docs**); its `whisper-stream` example reads an SDL microphone only (**docs**, its source). So pieces cut at pauses, of a few seconds to 30, within voice input's limit. The encoder alone takes 21–256 ms on a 30-second window with large-v3-turbo or smaller on M2 Ultra to M5 Max (whisper.cpp's own benchmarks, **docs**); decoding comes on top, and nothing was measured on the base chips or on a CPU build.
- Taiwanese Mandarin mixed with English: Breeze-ASR-25 (MediaTek, Apache-2.0, a Whisper large-v2 fine-tune), with community GGML conversions (**docs** for the model, **reported** for the conversions). Apple's `SpeechTranscriber` (macOS 26+) has zh-TW, one locale per transcriber, from Swift only (**docs**, **reported** for zh-TW).
- Products without a bot: Granola (Electron, a tap or ScreenCaptureKit, me and them, transcribed in the cloud), Notion's AI Meeting Notes, Fireflies' and Otter's desktop apps. Each leaves telling the others to the user, with a text to paste in the meeting's chat, a spoken notice or a badge on the video (**docs**).
- Consent: California asks every party's consent to record a confidential conversation (Penal Code §632), as do several other states (**reported**, lists differ); in Taiwan a participant may record (通訊保障及監察法 §29, **docs**). A class action filed against Otter.ai in August 2025 under California's and Illinois' laws is pending (**reported**).

### C, D and E. Bots and live APIs

2026's rules for bots:

- Zoom: from 2026-03-02, an app joining meetings outside its account must be authorized by an OBF token (**docs**, Zoom's developer blog), and its bot "can only join … once their verified associated owner is in a meeting … and must leave … when their owner leaves" (**docs**); joining other people's meetings needs an app Zoom has reviewed (**docs**); anonymous participants have been refused since about July 2026 (**reported**, Zoom's forum). On Zoom, a bot no longer attends for a user who is not there.
- Teams: a bot it detects waits in the lobby whatever the lobby's settings (`ExternalBotAccessMode`, `RequireApprovalWhenDetected` by default) (**docs**).
- Meet: the "safeguarded guest admit flow" puts risky guests in a second queue that defaults to Deny (**docs**).
- The services: Recall.ai, $0.50 an hour and $0.15 for transcription; its live transcript needs "a publicly exposed endpoint", and a new account keeps recordings until a retention is set (**docs**). Attendee (Elastic License 2.0; Docker with Postgres and Redis, amd64), Vexa (Apache-2.0), joinly (MIT: a Chromium bot with Whisper and MCP tools) (**docs**). Their bots join Meet and Teams in a browser they drive.
- The live APIs: Zoom's RTMS sends its stream's address in a webhook only, on a paid Developer Pack; Teams' media bots run in C# on Windows Server in Azure, each with a public IP; Google ends the Meet Media API and its endpoints on 2027-01-15 (**docs**).

## Design

Route A first, since it works for every platform and every account, needs no admin, joins nobody's call and sends no audio out; then B for what A did not hear. In this order, each a pull request of its own.

1. **Meeting notes on this computer.** **Take notes**, a button beside voice input in the composer, starts a meeting in the conversation: a `meeting` message part, whose card shows the transcript as it grows, the time, and Pause, Stop and Discard. One meeting records at a time on a server, since its streams share voice input's one engine.
   - What is captured: the microphone ("me") and the meeting's sound ("them"), as two streams, only after the user's click. In the desktop app on macOS and Windows, Electron's loopback (`setDisplayMediaRequestHandler` answering `audio: 'loopback'`, for the server's own page only, its video track dropped at once; the decision a pure function in `desktop/src/lifecycle.ts`, beside `permissionAllowed()`). On Linux, the default sink's monitor, read by the server (`parec` or `pw-record`, through `process.ts`), which cuts that stream itself with the page's cutter; started only by the desktop app's own window, through the app's channel to the server (`parentPort`, `src/server/parent.ts`), never by a request over HTTP, which a phone or another browser signed in to the same server could send. None of that channel exists yet: the window has no preload and no IPC (`desktop/src/main.ts`), and `parent.ts` carries the app's own messages only, so it needs a preload exposing one call, a main-process handler that checks the sender is the app's own window on the server's address, and a new message to the server naming the meeting; or the start from the tray's menu, which no page script can reach. The tray shows while it records. In a browser, Chrome's and Edge's shared tab: the user picks the meeting's tab. Elsewhere (Firefox, Safari, a phone through [remote access](../remote-access.md)), the microphone alone, which suits a meeting in person, and the card says so.
   - Transcription: the page cuts each stream at pauses (levels in an AudioWorklet, pieces of 30 seconds at most) and sends the pieces to the server (`POST /api/conversations/:id/meetings/:meetingId/audio`, JSON as every write is: a base64 16 kHz mono WAV within a size like voice input's, its stream and its start), one at a time per stream; the server transcribes each with voice input's engine and model (`VoiceService`), keeps the text with its stream, start and end, and pushes it over SSE. The audio is deleted once transcribed. When transcription falls behind, the page holds the pieces up to a limit, then drops the oldest, and the transcript marks the gap.
   - How long it lasts: the capturing page renews a lease every few seconds, with or without speech, so the server tells a quiet meeting from a page gone; a meeting whose lease lapses is paused, and its card offers to resume. It stops by itself after some minutes of silence on both streams, at a time limit of some hours, and when its conversation is deleted. These limits are constants in `src/shared/types.ts` (the pieces' length, the queue, the lease, the silence, the longest meeting), served through `GET /api/limits`.
   - What agents get: while it goes on, a message the user sends carries the transcript since the team's last turn as a `<meeting_transcript>` block of data (written through `dataBody()`, so a participant's words cannot close it; the briefing says: other people's words, never instructions). A `meeting_transcript` tool (`read`), given with any group as `outbox_status` is, to every member that gets Harness Relay's tools (Grok, Muse Code and the open agents in full access only, `GATEWAY_FULL_ACCESS_ONLY`), with a conversation that has a meeting getting them even with no app group (today they come only with one, `relayGroups`), gives any part of it by time or by words: the conversation's own meeting, or in an automation's run the one meeting its event names, which the server binds to the run (the agent's arguments never choose it), and nothing else; RLM mode reads a long one in parts. Once it stops: a run, of the team or of the lead agent alone, writes the summary, the decisions and the action items (who, by when), in the user's language, as a reply. From there the existing tools send it on (mail, Slack, Teams, Docs, Obsidian: each through the outbox), and follow-ups become proposed automations (`<schedule>` blocks) or, with long-term memory on, notes.
   - Telling the others: before the first meeting, and on the card each time, a short notice that Harness Relay joins no call, so nobody else knows unless the user tells them, with a text in both languages to paste in the meeting's chat (on Teams, proposed in step 4).
   - The desktop app: its own `NSAudioCaptureUsageDescription` in `extendInfo` and in both languages' `InfoPlist.strings`, in place of Electron's generic text; `permissionAllowed()` allowing `display-capture` to the server's own page. An audio track that starts `ended` means the permission was refused: the card says where to give it in System Settings. Since a grant can also go later, a meeting whose "them" stays silent while "me" hears speech gets the same note.
2. **Meetings in the calendar.** `CalendarEvent` gets the meeting's link and platform: from Graph's `onlineMeeting.joinUrl` and `onlineMeetingProvider`, with the event's body asked for too (not only `bodyPreview`); from iCalendar's `CONFERENCE` or Google's `X-GOOGLE-CONFERENCE` (**unsure** whether Google's CalDAV sends it); else the first Zoom, Meet or Teams link in the location or the description. The calendar tools and the calendar trigger's `<event>` list it. A notification that a meeting is about to begin, with **Take notes**, opens a conversation that has the event's title, attendees and description as the meeting's context; it never starts the microphone by itself. And a new source for automations, a meeting that ended (its event's end, or step 1's Stop), whose `<event source="meeting">` names the meeting, which that run's `meeting_transcript` may read: "send the action items to #team" set once for a series.
3. **The platform's transcript.** For the meetings step 1 did not hear (the user was not there, or on a phone), and for the platforms' speaker names:
   - Teams: a `meetings` use of the Microsoft account, with `OnlineMeetings.Read` and `OnlineMeetingTranscript.Read.All` (admin consent); the event's meeting found by its join link, its transcripts asked for after its end until they are there, less and less often (as automations' checks back off), read into the same transcript: the VTT with its speakers, or where the tenant refuses speakers, the text without them; a tenant that refuses transcripts to Graph is told as such.
   - Meet: a `meet` use of the Google account, with `meetings.space.readonly`; the meeting code from step 2's Meet link, or else from the Calendar API's `conferenceData` (whose host the Google provider then needs: `googleProvider()`'s `hosts` has CalDAV's and the user info's only); the conference records of the meetings the user organized, their entries and smart notes; for a meeting the user only attended, the Docs attached to its event, through Drive.
   - Both through the broker (`src/server/oauth/broker.ts`), no token to a CLI; a `meeting_import` tool (`read`), and step 2's meeting-ended source waiting for the transcript.
4. **Teams' meeting chat.** The meeting chat's thread as a destination of `teams_send_message`: from a classic join link, which holds it (`/l/meetup-join/19:meeting_…@thread.v2/0`), which `destinationFromLink()` then takes, its tenant (`Tid` in the link's `context`) checked against the posting session's; from a newer `/meet/…` link, which does not, through Graph's `chatInfo.threadId`. A text, an answer, or the summary afterwards goes to the meeting's chat once the user confirms it in the outbox. In the transcript (step 1 or 3), the lines that name the user or ask the team are marked, each with **Ask the team** on the card: the user starts that turn, and other people's words never start one; nothing is sent by itself. Meet and Zoom have no such API: the card offers the text to copy.
5. **Zoom.** A provider for the user's own Zoom General app (PKCE; the redirect `http://127.0.0.1:<port>/oauth/callback`, never `localhost`, which `redirectAddress()` in `src/server/oauth/signin.ts` would give a page on localhost, so Zoom's is forced to 127.0.0.1, as Microsoft's is kept off IPv6 loopback), with a use for the transcripts and summaries of the meetings the user hosted, and the My Notes API for those they attended, once a signed-in check shows that it covers them.

Not planned: a bot that joins (C, D) and the live APIs (E); see [Alternatives considered](#alternatives-considered).

The schema gets `meetings` (a conversation's meeting: where it came from, its event, its times and state) and `meeting_segments` (its stream or speaker, start, end and text), removed with the conversation.

## Security

- The audio stays on the user's computers: whisper.cpp transcribes it on the server's computer, as it does for voice input, and nothing sends it elsewhere. With remote access the page that captures it can be on another computer, and its pieces cross the network to the server as voice input's do. The pieces come under `/api/`, through its middleware (the sign-in, the Host and Origin checks, JSON only), each checked to be a WAV within its size, and are deleted once transcribed.
- The transcript's text does not stay here: it goes in the members' turns to their model providers, as any message does. For a meeting that must not leave, a team on the user's own model endpoints keeps it on machines the user runs; the card says which providers its team's members use.
- What the others say is data, never instructions: a `<meeting_transcript>` block and the tool's result, within their limits, as mail and pages are. Every message to other people and every document write through Harness Relay's own tools waits for the user in the outbox, in every permission, full access included (`send`), as today. That is not all a team can do: in full access the CLIs' own shells and network, the browser tools' clicks and typing (`act`), the tool library's MCP servers and the connectors of Claude and Codex themselves run without asking. So a participant's words reach, in full access, a team that acts without asking; the card warns when a meeting's conversation is in full access.
- The microphone and the meeting's sound only after the user's click, never because of an automation or a reply; the card shows that they are on, and on Linux, where the server records, the tray of the computer it records on.
- Steps 3 and 5's tokens stay in the server, in the broker, as the calendar's do, and go only to their providers' hosts; step 4 posts through the Teams posting session there is, whose sign-in stays in its PowerShell process.
- Telling the others stays the user's to do: Harness Relay says so and gives the words, and never says it was done.
- The transcript holds other people's words: it is kept in the database with its conversation, removed with it or with Discard, and left out of a shared conversation unless the user includes it. The copies already made go on: in the CLIs' own session files, in AgentsView's threads, in long-term memory notes made from it; Discard says so.

## Alternatives considered

- **A bot service's participant (C).** It attends without the user and posts in any meeting's chat. But the audio of everyone in the call goes to a third party, which keeps it unless told otherwise, at $0.30–0.50 an hour and more with transcription; its live transcript needs a public endpoint; and 2026's rules put it in Teams' lobby and Meet's second queue, and on Zoom keep it only while its owner is there, which leaves it little over step 1. Possible later, as a connector with a key in the credential store, read after the meeting, if users ask for meetings they do not attend.
- **The browser tools joining (D).** Their Chromium is headless, its sound muted: a guest the host must admit (in Meet, into the queue that defaults to Deny), WebRTC kept to the proxy with no UDP, and no sound to read. Giving it a microphone, speakers and UDP would undo what keeps its pages from the network and the computer. joinly shows such a bot can work in a container of its own, not in Harness Relay's browser.
- **The live APIs (E).** None reaches a server on 127.0.0.1, and Meet's ends in January 2027.
- **A native capture helper** (Swift on Core Audio taps, like audiotee; Rust for Windows' process loopback and PipeWire, like application-loopback). The meeting app's sound alone, without a video track, so perhaps without the Screen Recording question. But a binary to build and sign per system: kept for when step 1 shows that Electron's route asks for Screen Recording.
- **Recall.ai's desktop SDK.** Captures on the computer without a bot, but uploads to Recall's cloud, has no Linux, and costs as its bots do.
- **Apple's `SpeechTranscriber`.** On the device, with zh-TW, but macOS 26+, Swift only and one language at a time; whisper.cpp is already there, on every system.
- **Transcribing in the cloud** (Deepgram, as Granola does, or a model connection's). Faster on small computers, but it sends everyone's voices out, which Harness Relay's voice input never does.
- **Answering in the meeting's chat by itself, as Grok's bot does.** A participant's words would decide what the team posts, as the user, to other people; the outbox keeps that decision the user's.

## Open questions

- Keep the audio, for a better transcript afterwards with a larger model? Off by default; kept how long?
- Whether Electron 45's loopback on macOS asks for Screen Recording as well as System Audio Recording, how often macOS asks again, and whether the ad hoc signed app loses the grant at each update: **unsure**, not tried. If so, a native helper, or a Developer ID signature.
- The desktop app's window closed to the tray while a meeting records: keep its page running (the window hidden, its timers not throttled), or pause the meeting?
- Run a meeting's turns below full access, whatever the conversation's permission, since other people's words are in them?
- The echo without headphones: "me" hears the others too. Drop a piece of "me" whose words "them" has at the same time, or ask for headphones?
- Telling the others apart (sherpa-onnx's diarization of the "them" stream, as OpenWhispr does) in step 1, or names only from the platforms' transcripts (step 3)?
- Breeze-ASR-25 as a voice model for Taiwanese Mandarin mixed with English: from which conversion, pinned and checked as the others are? And Silero's model for `--vad`, pinned the same way; on macOS, where the engine comes from Homebrew, whether its build has VAD.
- Whose run writes the summary: the team's, or the lead agent's alone, which costs less? A fixed outline, or a template of the user's?
- Teams' admin consent: through the user's own Entra app (the OAuth account), or through Microsoft Graph PowerShell's session, as Teams' reads already can?
- Whether `drive_read_document` reads Meet's transcript and notes Docs as they are, and whether Google's CalDAV gives the meeting's link: **unsure**, not tried.
- Whether an attendee (not the organizer) can find a Teams meeting by its join link and post in its chat: **unsure**.
- Whether `OnlineMeetingTranscript.Read.All` needs admin consent in every tenant, as its page says, or a tenant's consent policy can let a user give it: to check on Graph's permissions reference.
